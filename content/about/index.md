# Hi, I'm Titus

I work in product data at SGS Engineering, where I help make sense of the information behind the products customers see and buy online.

My work sits somewhere between **data, ecommerce, products, and commercial operations**. Depending on the day, I might be analysing sales performance, building product bundles, managing pricing structures, improving product attributes, working on SEO, or merchandising products across the website.

I like being the person who can step back, understand how those pieces connect, and turn something complicated into something that works.

# How I Got Here

The path here wasn't exactly planned.

Growing up, sport was a big part of my life. I played a lot of rugby and eventually made it into both the **Harlequins** and **London Broncos** academies, with the aim of playing professionally.

A series of concussions, followed by COVID, eventually brought that chapter to an end.

It left me having to figure out what came next.

During a careers meeting, I discovered that you could actually study sport at university. That sounded like a pretty good idea at the time, so I ended up at **Nottingham Trent University**.

It turned out to be a more useful decision than I realised.

University introduced me to research methods, statistical analysis, biomechanics, physiology, and evidence-based decision making. Somewhere along the way, I realised I particularly enjoyed the analytical side of things — taking a messy set of information, working through it, and eventually getting to an answer.

I remember one piece of coursework in particular. A group of us were in the library working through the results section, surrounded by datasets and statistical output. I put my head down and worked through mine in a few hours.

I later discovered some of my classmates had spent weeks on it.

That was probably one of the first signs that this kind of work came quite naturally to me. More importantly, I realised I actually enjoyed doing it.

## From sport to data

During university, a lecture from a visiting Sports Data Analyst opened my eyes to the fact that data could actually be a career in sport.

That led to a placement with **Nova Centurion Swimming Club**, where I got the chance to work with data in the environment of elite athletes.

Alongside university, I was also working at **Halfords**.

I started building bicycles on weekends at 16. During COVID, when much of the country had stopped working, I became a full-time essential worker, taking on everything from bike builds and repairs to customers and stock control.

I continued working there throughout university, eventually becoming the Cycle Tech before progressing to Department Manager shortly after finishing my final-year exams.

It was a very different environment from university research and sports data, but it taught me a lot about how businesses actually operate.

I learned about customers, stock, operations, targets, people, commercial decisions, and — perhaps most importantly — solving problems when things don't go according to plan.

Looking back, those experiences ended up being much more connected than I initially thought.

### Realising I liked the analysis more than the subject

After university, I started looking for roles in sports data. I had a good job as a Department Manager, but I didn't see myself doing it long-term.

There was one problem: there weren't many sports data roles.

And the ones that did exist often wanted several years of experience for what was already a relatively niche career path.

Eventually, I started questioning what it was I actually enjoyed about sports data.

The answer wasn't necessarily **sport**.

It was **analysis**.

I liked taking information, finding patterns, understanding what was happening, and using that understanding to make something better.

That realisation opened things up considerably.

Not long after, I interviewed at **SGS Engineering**. What initially looked like an unusual combination of experiences — data analysis and several years working in retail — turned out to be surprisingly relevant.

I joined SGS and found myself working at the intersection of data, ecommerce, products, and commercial operations.

And that's where I am now.

# What Now?

My role has opened up a number of possible directions — product data, PIM management, commercial analysis, ecommerce, and technical product roles among them.

I'm not particularly interested in forcing myself into one path just for the sake of having one.

For now, I'm more interested in building useful skills, working on interesting problems, and seeing where that takes me.

This site is the record of that journey.
